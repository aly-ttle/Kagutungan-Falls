About

History of Kagutungan Falls

The name Kagutongan traces its roots to the Bagbag-o dialect, where the word "gutong" refers to a large rock. These massive rock formations are found abundantly in the area, and they played a key role in shaping its identity.

Barangay Daligan, home to members of the Indigenous Peoples (IP) community, holds this name with cultural pride. According to the Bagbag-o people, the term gutong is specifically used for large rocks where in smaller stones are not referred to by this name.

It was the ancient inhabitants of Daligan who named the place Kagutongan, inspired by the striking presence of these giant rocks. Over time, the area became known not only for its rocky terrain but also for its stunning waterfalls. Thus, the name evolved into Kagutongan Falls, celebrating both its geological and natural beauty.

Kagutungan Falls sits along the Kagutungan river in the hills east of Barangay Daligan, Santa Cruz, Ilocos Sur. Locally, the falls is also known by the name Ban-aw Lawa. It has long been a favorite spot among residents of Santa Cruz, and has since opened up to visitors from outside the municipality.

The falls and its basin

The water drops in tiers over a rock face into a wide basin at the bottom. The basin is calm and deep enough for swimming, and a number of visitors use the surrounding rock ledges for cliff-jumping. Despite some development on-site, the forest around the falls remains largely intact.

What's on site

The barangay has added a few comforts for visitors: concrete gazebos, a comfort room, and resting huts near the top of the falls. These make Kagutungan a reasonably comfortable day trip even though reaching it still requires a proper trek.

Nearby landmarks

Visitors with extra time and energy can continue past Kagutungan Falls to see two more natural attractions in the area:

Sinminublan Pool

A second natural pool further along the trail, reachable with additional walking time beyond the falls.

Sinmimbaan Cave

A cave landmark near Sinminublan Pool, best visited with a local guide given the added distance from the falls.

Good to know

All visitors must register for entry and pay 20 pesos at the basketball court in Barangay Daligan before heading to the falls. A tour guide is optional but recommended, especially for first-time visitors or those planning to continue on to Sinminublan Pool or Sinmimbaan Cave.
